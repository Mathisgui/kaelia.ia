You probably already use ChatGPT. To draft a tricky email, summarise a document that is too long, sketch the outline of a proposal. And one question keeps coming up among the business owners we meet: is AI for business simply that, at a larger scale? A subscription for the whole team and a few good habits? The answer is no. The difference comes down to one word: access. ChatGPT cannot see your tools. An AI agent can. Everything else follows from there.

## What ChatGPT does really well

Let us be clear from the start: ChatGPT is an excellent tool, and if your team already uses it, that is rather a good sign. It shines at everything involving text and ideas:

- **Writing**: a sales email, a job ad, a LinkedIn post.
- **Rephrasing**: making a message clearer, shorter or more diplomatic.
- **Summarising**: meeting notes, a twenty-page document, a long email thread.
- **Brainstorming**: name ideas, likely objections, angles for an offer.

The gain is real, but it is individual: each person gets faster at their own writing and thinking tasks. That is already a lot. It is simply not the same thing as AI integrated into the business.

## Where ChatGPT stops

Used on its own, ChatGPT has four structural limits for a business:

- **It cannot see your tools.** Your CRM, your inbox, your invoices, your calendar: it has no access to them. Every piece of information has to be brought to it by hand, copy-pasted, in every conversation.
- **It does not act.** It answers, it suggests, it drafts. But it does not send the email, update the customer record or file the invoice. Execution stays entirely on your side.
- **Everyone starts from scratch.** No shared rules, no common way of doing things: two colleagues asking the same question get two different answers, and nobody can trace what was done or why.
- **Copy-pasted data leaves your perimeter.** Whatever goes into a consumer chat leaves your tools and your control. For a quote, a contract or customer data, the question at least deserves to be asked.

None of this is a flaw in ChatGPT: it is a tool designed for conversation, not for execution. The trouble starts when it is asked to play a role that was never its own.

## What an AI agent changes

An AI agent runs on the same kind of intelligence, but installed inside your work environment rather than next to it. In practice:

- **It is connected to your tools.** Inbox, CRM, invoicing, calendar: it reads and writes directly in the software you already use.
- **It works within a defined scope.** It does not do everything: it handles one precise task, scoped with you, with written rules.
- **It performs real actions**: filing a document, entering an order, following up with a customer, generating a draft reply in the right place.
- **It asks for human validation** on sensitive actions: nothing goes out to a customer without your approval, unless you decide otherwise.
- **It leaves a trail.** Every action is logged: you know what was done, when, and on which file.

That is the difference in kind: ChatGPT is AI you talk to, an agent is AI that works. Our [AI agents](/en/ai-agents) page details the most common use cases in small businesses.

## Three situations, side by side

Three ordinary moments in the life of a small business, seen from both sides:

- **A customer email arrives** with a question about an order. ChatGPT helps you write a good reply, provided you copy the email in and dig up the information yourself. The agent reads the email, finds the order in your tools, prepares the reply and submits it to you for sending.
- **A supplier invoice comes in** as an attachment. ChatGPT can extract the amounts if you pass it the document. The agent detects it on arrival, extracts the data, enters it into your accounting tool and files the document in the right place.
- **A quote has gone unanswered** for ten days. ChatGPT writes an excellent follow-up, if you remember to ask for it. The agent watches pending quotes, triggers the follow-up at the right moment and records everything in the CRM.

The pattern is the same in all three cases: ChatGPT improves what you do when you think of it; the agent makes sure it gets done, every time, without depending on anyone's memory.

## And what about the price?

The two do not play in the same budget category, and that is normal:

- **ChatGPT** costs around 20 € per month per person for the paid version. For everyday writing help, it is a very good investment.
- **A custom AI agent** is built for a fixed price, from 1,500 € depending on the scope, plus a few dozen euros of API costs per month to run.

So the comparison is not about the subscription, but about the time saved: an agent makes sense when a recurring task represents several hours per week. ## The takeaway

ChatGPT and an AI agent do not answer the same question. The first helps each person write and think better; the second takes over a business process, inside your tools, with rules and a trail. The two complement each other very well: keep ChatGPT for everyday writing, and consider an agent the day a repetitive task starts costing real hours every week.
