AI agents for tradespeople do not replace the trade itself or the relationship with a client, they change three specific moments in a working day: the quote, the paperwork, and part of the replies to clients. Eight tradespeople out of ten see no use for it, and that skepticism often comes from demos that look nothing like a real job site.

## Why is tradespeople's skepticism justified?

According to [France Num's report on AI in the trades](https://www.francenum.gouv.fr/guides-et-conseils/intelligence-artificielle/comprendre-et-adopter-lia/lintelligence-artificielle-2), 27 percent of business owners surveyed say they use an AI tool in their work. In construction, the picture is even clearer: according to the latest CCCA-BTP barometers cited in that same report, only 7 percent of construction companies surveyed in 2024 had already used an AI-related solution, and 9 percent regretted not doing so. 81.5 percent of companies do not feel these tools are useful, and 3 percent say they do not know what they are.

That number is not a gap to force closed. Most tradespeople who have seen an AI demo have seen a generic chatbot or a writing tool, nothing that looks like a plumber's quote or a subcontracting declaration. Taking that refusal seriously, rather than dismissing it, means recognizing the problem is not the tradesperson but the gap between what gets shown to them and what actually fills their days.

## What tradespeople who adopted it get out of it

Among the 27 percent already using an AI tool, the most common uses are creating and updating communication materials, for 81 percent of them, and client relations, for 24 percent. The same France Num report measures a time gain of 2.1 hours per week, about 5 percent productivity, plus two satisfaction figures: 84 percent of users report saving time, 87 percent say the quality of the completed task improved.

These figures remain an average across all tasks, measured on a population that already chose to adopt a tool. The safest way to read them is as a ceiling rather than a promise: a business with a lot of repetitive paperwork gets closer to it, one that already invoices through an integrated piece of software gets less out of it.

## The quote, the first place it actually changes something

The quote stays the place where lost time shows up fastest, because it comes back every week and almost always follows the same structure from one client to the next.

<Scenario>
A three person roofing business that puts out around thirty quotes a month, written up in the evening after a day on site. The client request comes from a call or a text exchange, prices sit in a spreadsheet, and every quote reuses the same template with quantities and surfaces changing. An agent that reads the exchange, looks up prices in the spreadsheet, and prepares a first figure frees up exactly that evening slot, the one where fatigue causes the most line or discount errors.
</Scenario>

## Paperwork: electronic invoicing is coming fast

Paperwork is not limited to quotes. According to the [France Num 2025 barometer](https://www.francenum.gouv.fr/guides-et-conseils/strategie-numerique/comprendre-le-numerique/barometre-france-num-2025-le), receiving invoices in electronic format becomes mandatory starting September 1, 2026 for the businesses concerned, and construction arrives fairly well equipped: 76 percent of construction companies already have invoicing software, against 69 percent of all French small and medium businesses, up two points in a year.

| Indicator | Construction | All small and medium businesses |
|---|---|---|
| Equipped with invoicing software | 76 percent | 69 percent |
| Invoices issued in structured format | not broken out by sector | 20 percent |
| Electronic invoice receipt deadline | September 1, 2026 | September 1, 2026 |

Having invoicing software does not mean issuing invoices in the structured format the reform requires: only 20 percent of businesses surveyed, across all sizes, already do that. That gap is often where the real work sits before the deadline, more than in picking a new tool.

## What is not worth it on a job site?

Nothing replaces hands on training, the eye that spots a flaw in a finish, or the trust built with a client over years. An AI agent does not climb on the roof, does not negotiate a tight deadline with an unhappy client, and does not decide on a fix when something unexpected turns up on site. Trying to automate the client relationship itself, rather than its preparation, produces generic messages that show from a mile away and damage trust more than they save time.

AI prepares repetitive administrative tasks, it never replaces trade judgment or the final call, which stay entirely with the tradesperson.

## How do you start without losing a month to it?

A first step fits in one hour, with no outside help:

- list the five or six most recent quotes,
- note what repeats from one client to the next: same categories, same discounts,
- check whether prices always come from the same source, a spreadsheet or a catalog.
