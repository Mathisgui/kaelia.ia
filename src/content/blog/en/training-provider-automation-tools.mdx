A training provider runs on a stack of tools that rarely talk to each other: Digiforma or a similar platform for session management, spreadsheets for tracking funding and quality indicators, and an inbox that centralizes everything else. Nobody wants to rebuild the whole stack just to save time. The good news is that automation does not require switching tools: it plugs into what you already have to move information around without re-typing it. Here is what fits well, and what should stay driven by a person.

## Why plug into the existing stack rather than replace it

Switching training management software is a heavy, costly project, and often a risky one a few months before a compliance audit. Most providers do not need a new tool, they need the tools they already use to stop generating repetitive manual work: copying an enrollment from an email into Digiforma, updating a tracking spreadsheet by hand, chasing trainees one by one to collect signed attendance sheets. An automation sits between these tools, through their APIs where they exist, or by reading email and files where they do not, without touching what already works.

This is the same principle described in our article on [tasks SMBs can automate](/en/blog/tasks-smb-can-automate): look first for what is repetitive and rule-based, before thinking about a new tool.

## Tracking enrollments and funding files

An enrollment rarely comes through a single channel: a website form, a direct email, a call from a funding body. A [document AI agent](/en/ai-document-agent) can read these different sources, extract the trainee's and session's information, then create or update the matching record in Digiforma without re-typing anything. When a funding file needs to be put together, the agent can also pre-fill the recurring fields from information already known about the trainee or the company, leaving the final check to the staff member before it is sent.

The gain shows up most during busy periods, at year end or ahead of a new intake, when the volume of enrollments outpaces what one person can process without mistakes.

## Reminders for compliance documents

Collecting the documents needed for a quality compliance file, attendance sheets, hot and cold evaluations, completion certificates, looks a lot like the missing-document problem we describe elsewhere on this blog for accounting firms. An automation can track collection status per session, send an automatic reminder to the relevant trainer or trainee, then escalate to the pedagogical manager once the deadline has passed. This is very close to what we cover in our article on [automated client follow-ups](/en/blog/automated-client-follow-ups), applied here to the compliance calendar rather than to invoicing.

Consistency matters more than speed on this kind of task: an automation never lets a reminder slip because the week got busy, which meaningfully cuts the risk of an incomplete file on audit day.

## Reporting indicators into the tracking spreadsheet

Many providers track their quality indicators, satisfaction rate, dropout rate, placement rate, in a spreadsheet filled in by hand from Digiforma data and evaluation responses. An [AI reporting agent](/en/ai-reporting-agent) can aggregate this data automatically on a regular schedule and update the spreadsheet or a dashboard, instead of someone compiling the numbers every month. The automation does not replace human analysis of the result, it just removes a recurring half-day of number-crunching.

## What should stay in a person's hands

The decision to accept or decline a funding file, the pedagogical evaluation of a trainee, or the conversation with a compliance auditor should still go through a professional at the organization. Automation prepares documents, collects files, formats the indicators, but it does not sign off on anything in place of a pedagogical or administrative manager. It is the same control logic we describe for the [AI sales agent](/en/ai-sales-agent): the AI prepares, the human decides.

## What this type of project costs

An automation limited to a single process, for example automatic reminders for missing compliance documents, typically starts around 1,500 to 2,500 euros as a fixed-fee project, with a few dozen euros a month in running costs for the APIs involved. A project that touches document reading and data extraction, such as multi-channel enrollment tracking, tends to fall between 2,500 and 5,000 euros. Ongoing maintenance then runs between 100 and 200 euros a month if you want us to keep monitoring the system, or it can be taken over internally if someone at the organization is comfortable with the tools involved.

These figures remain orders of magnitude, with a caution discount applied to estimated gains: a provider with a single platform and homogeneous processes does not have the same scope as one juggling several tools and several funding bodies.

## The takeaway

Automating a training provider does not require switching software: it means plugging an automation into Digiforma, your spreadsheets, and your inbox to remove re-typing and manual reminders, while keeping decisions and quality control in a person's hands. A first targeted project, on compliance document reminders or enrollment tracking, typically runs between 1,500 and 5,000 euros depending on complexity.
