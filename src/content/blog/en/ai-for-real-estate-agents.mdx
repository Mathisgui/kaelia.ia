What AI for real estate agents can actually do goes beyond listings and property valuations, whatever most content on the topic suggests. For a professional working alone, the real gain sits in following a network of several hundred contacts, too many to hold in one head, and in everything that happens between two appointments.

## Where AI genuinely saves time in real estate

Most content on this topic stops at two uses: generating listing copy and automated property valuation. Both are real, but already covered by most industry software, so they offer little edge to anyone still searching for them.

According to the [France Num 2025 barometer](https://www.francenum.gouv.fr/guides-et-conseils/strategie-numerique/comprendre-le-numerique/barometre-france-num-2025-le), across all business activities and with no figure specific to real estate, the share of small and mid-sized businesses using AI has doubled in a year to reach 26 percent. Usage is dominated by generative AI (22 percent, up 12 points) and chatbots and assistants (14 percent, up 9 points), well ahead of document analysis (6 percent) or task automation (5 percent).

| Task | Real gain | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Listing copy | First draft from the property's characteristics | Review required, already covered by industry software |
| Contact follow-up | Reminders and monitoring without depending on one person's memory | Human approval before anything reaches a client |
| Document analysis | Fast lookups in a mandate or a survey report | Never carries legal judgment |
| First inquiries | Sorting and drafting a reply | Fine qualification stays human |

## The real subject: following a portfolio of contacts

A professional working alone who tracks several hundred active contacts cannot remember, for each one, what stage they are at, when they were last in touch, or which newly listed property might suit them. Today, that tracking depends on a spreadsheet, a poorly maintained CRM, or a memory that eventually gives out.

## Should AI be allowed to write to clients?

No, not without human approval. A message going out to an investor or a seller carries your reputation, and a generative AI can get an amount, a date, or a property detail wrong without it being obvious at first glance.

The right practice is the one we describe in [how to stay in control of an AI agent](/en/blog/securing-ai-agents-business): a defined scope, human approval on anything leaving the business, and a record of what was sent. This matters even more in real estate, since a contact file holds sensitive personal data (income, purchase plans, family situation), a point the [CNIL's practical AI factsheets](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/les-fiches-pratiques-ia) flag for any system that processes this kind of data.

## What AI will not do in your place

Three things do not get delegated, whatever tool you choose:

- **The viewing.** Feeling out a place and answering a hesitation on the spot cannot be automated.
- **The negotiation.** It requires reading a human situation in real time and adjusting a position, something no system does reliably.
- **The trust.** What a seller or an investor places in a person rather than a tool is built over time and delegates to nothing.

Content that promised otherwise would be selling a solution that does not exist. Saying so plainly is what keeps the rest of the article credible.

## How much does a project cost, and how long does it take?

A first project focused on contact follow-up starts at 1,500 euros as a flat fee, never a daily rate, depending on how many existing tools need connecting and how wide the agreed scope is. Running costs then stay limited to a few dozen euros a month in API calls.

The time saved scales directly with the size of the portfolio tracked, and it is better announced below what is actually observed than above it: a professional disappointed by an inflated promise does not come back. For a portfolio of several hundred contacts, the time recovered is measured in hours per week, not minutes, but that figure varies too much from one file to another to state as a general rule.
