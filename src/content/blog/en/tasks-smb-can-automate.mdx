Every small business loses time on repetitive tasks, but few know which ones to automate first. The good news: the best candidates are rarely spectacular. They are the mundane tasks, the ones that come back every single day. Here are the 10 we see most often, what an automation can actually do with them, and a simple method to choose the one to start with.

## Why start small

The temptation, once you discover what AI agents can do, is to automate everything at once. That is the surest way to fail: too many changes at the same time, a team that disengages, results impossible to measure. Automate one task at a time. You see the real gain, the team adopts the tool, and you decide what comes next with numbers rather than intuitions.

A good candidate is easy to recognise, with two criteria: high volume (the task comes back every day or every week) and clear rules (the steps can be described precisely, without an exception on every line). The 10 tasks below tick both boxes in most companies.

## The 10 tasks

In the order we recommend them most often, documents first:

1. **Entering incoming invoices and quotes.** An AI agent reads the PDFs arriving by email, extracts the amounts, dates and references, then records them in your accounting tool or spreadsheet. High volume, stable rules, costly data entry errors: this is often the best starting point.
2. **Updating the CRM after every exchange.** After a call or an email, the automation creates or completes the client record and summarises the exchange. The CRM stays up to date without anyone having to think about it, and follow-ups rely on fresh information.
3. **Chasing quotes that got no reply.** A quote that is never followed up is often a quote lost. The automation follows up at the right moment with a personalised message and stops as soon as the client replies.
4. **Sorting incoming emails.** Each message is classified by type of request and routed to the right person, with a drafted reply for the common cases. On a shared inbox, the gain is measured in hours per week.
5. **Generating the monthly report.** The numbers already exist in your tools: the automation gathers them, formats them and sends the report on a fixed date. Many small businesses recover half a day per month here.
6. **Onboarding new clients.** Welcome email, folder creation, access setup, first meeting scheduled: the sequence is identical every time, which makes it perfectly automatable.
7. **Filing and naming documents.** Every incoming document is renamed according to your conventions and stored in the right place. Tiny per document, considerable over a year.
8. **Reading scanned forms and sheets (OCR).** The AI reads scanned paper documents and turns them into usable data. A training organisation, for example, can process its attendance sheets without any retyping.
9. **Answering recurring internal questions.** An assistant grounded in your documents answers the everyday questions: where to find the contract template, how to request time off, which procedure applies to a given type of client. The most solicited people on the team can breathe again.
10. **Sending satisfaction surveys.** After each service or delivery, the survey goes out automatically, the answers are compiled and negative feedback is flagged to you first.

## How to choose the first one

Three criteria are enough to separate the candidates:

- **Volume.** A 15-minute task repeated every day weighs more than a one-hour task repeated every month. Aim for at least 3 to 4 hours per week on the task you pick.
- **Stability.** If your way of working changes every month, wait until the process settles. An automation built on a moving process spends its life in maintenance.
- **Tools.** Check that your software offers an API or at least an export. That is what keeps the integration simple and the project affordable.

Before deciding, measure for one week: how often the task comes back, how long it takes each time. Then keep 70 to 80 percent of the measured time, because an automation never captures everything, there are always edge cases and a bit of supervision. That prudent number is the basis of an honest profitability calculation, which we detail in our article on [calculating the ROI of an automation](/en/blog/calculate-automation-roi).

On the budget side, a custom project starts from 1,500 € fixed price, plus a few dozen euros of API costs per month. With a fully loaded hourly cost of 25 to 40 € in a small business, a task worth 3 to 4 hours per week generally pays for itself in under a year, often within a few months.

## The takeaway

You do not need a big transformation programme to save time: a single one of these 10 tasks, well chosen, frees up several hours per week and proves the value of automation in your own context. Start with the one that combines the highest volume, stable rules and open tools. Measure for a week, run the numbers, then launch.
